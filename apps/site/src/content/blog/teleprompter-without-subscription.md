## Why subscriptions annoy creators

Nobody minds paying for software they use every day. What people mind is a monthly charge for something they use twice a quarter, a trial that silently converts, or a "pro" tier that fences off the one feature that made the app worth opening.

Teleprompters are especially exposed to this because usage is bursty. You record a course over two weeks and then nothing for a month. A subscription prices that as if you recorded every day. It also creates the worst possible failure mode: the card expires the morning of the shoot and the app locks the script you wrote last night.

The pushback is reasonable, and it is why so many people search for teleprompters without a subscription.

## Free options

Free teleprompters fall into two groups. Some are free because they are a hobby project or a loss-leader for something else; those are often excellent but can disappear. Others are free because they are open source, which means the code outlives any one maintainer.

Things worth checking in a free tool:

- **Does it scroll with your voice or only with a timer?** Fixed-speed scrolling is free everywhere and has been since the first browser prompter; voice following is the feature that changes how you read. Our guide to [[how-a-voice-following-teleprompter-works]] explains how to tell the two apart in a minute.
- **Where do scripts go?** A free web tool that stores scripts on its server is paying its bills somehow. Prefer tools that keep text in your browser.
- **Does it work offline?** An installable web app that keeps working on a plane is more useful than a page that needs the server up.

The gotoprompter web app is in the second group: MIT-licensed, scripts stored locally, installable, voice following included. It is the complete product for anyone who does not need a floating window on a Mac.

## Pay-once options

A pay-once licence is the traditional desktop model, and for a focused tool it still makes the most sense. You pay, you get a key, the app keeps working whether or not the company has a good quarter.

The questions to ask before buying:

- **How many machines?** A personal licence typically covers two or three computers. Check that you can deactivate an old machine yourself rather than emailing support.
- **What happens if the company stops?** A licence that must phone home every day is only as durable as the server. Look for a generous offline grace period.
- **Are updates included?** Some pay-once apps charge for major versions. That is legitimate if it is stated up front, and misleading if it is not.
